The rules & your privacy

Short version first. The full documents are behind the tabs.

Who can use Turnout

You need to be 16 or older. Turnout is for finding real parties with real people. Don’t use it if you’re younger.

What we store

Your name, ZIP, email, and profile picture. If you host, the party’s address. When you check in, the fact that you were there and roughly when. Photos you put in an album or send in a message.

Who can see it

A party address is only readable by the host and people holding a ticket — it is locked in the database, not just hidden in the app. Album photos are only visible to people who checked in at that party. Messages and the photos in them are only visible to you and the person you sent them to. Your home address, if you save one, is never shown to anyone.

Location

Check-in compares your location to the host’s spot on your phone and sends the result, not a trail of where you’ve been. Party pins on the map are deliberately fuzzed until you’re in.

What’s not allowed

Fake parties, harassment, posting someone’s address without permission, photos of people who don’t want to be posted, and anything illegal. Report anyone doing it — every profile and party has a report option, and blocking someone cuts contact both ways.

Parties are not ours

Turnout helps you find a party. It doesn’t run it, vouch for it, or take responsibility for what happens there. Use your judgment, tell someone where you’re going, and look out for each other.

Deleting your stuff

Profile → Delete my account removes your account, your parties, your photos and your messages for good.

Privacy Policy · last updated 31 August 2026

Turnout is a small app for finding house parties, run by Cade DeLuca in Missouri. This explains exactly what it stores about you, who can see it, and how to get rid of it. It is written to be read, not to be skipped.

What you give us
  • To make an account: your email address, a password, the name you want shown, and your ZIP code.
  • Optional: a profile photo, and a home address that auto-fills the host form.
  • When you host: the party’s name, times, cover, capacity and street address.
  • When you go out: which parties you claimed a spot at, whether you checked in and roughly when, photos you add to a party album, messages and reactions you send, who your friends are, private nicknames you give them, ratings you leave a host, and reports you file.
  • If you turn on notifications: a push token for that device, so your phone can be buzzed. Turn it off and it is deleted.
Location

Location is off unless you switch it on. When you check in, your phone compares where you are to the host’s spot and sends the answer — near enough, or not — not a trail of where you have been. Party pins on the map are deliberately imprecise until you are holding a ticket.

Who can see what
  • Anyone signed in: your display name and profile photo.
  • Only people holding a ticket to that party: its street address.
  • Only people who were actually there: that party’s album photos and who checked in.
  • Only you and the other person: your messages.
  • Only you, ever: your saved home address, and the nicknames you give friends.
  • Nobody, individually: tapping “I’m out tonight” only ever shows other people a count. Your name is never in that list.

These are not just rules in the app — they are enforced by the database itself, on every single request, so a modified app cannot read around them.

Who else touches your data
  • Supabase — hosts the database, accounts and photo storage (United States).
  • OpenStreetMap Nominatim and Zippopotam — an address or ZIP is sent to turn it into map coordinates or a town name.
  • Esri / ArcGIS — draws the map tiles, and sees your IP address doing it.
  • Cloudflare, jsDelivr, Google Fonts — serve code and fonts; they see your IP.
  • Apple, Google or Mozilla’s push service — only if you turned notifications on, and only to deliver the buzz.
What we never do

There are no ads, no advertising trackers, no analytics SDKs, and no data brokers. Nothing about you is sold or rented, and nothing is shared with anyone except the services above that are needed to make the app work — or if the law actually requires it.

How long it is kept, and deleting it

Your data stays until you remove it. Profile → Delete my account permanently deletes your account, your profile, your parties and their addresses, your photos, your messages, your friendships and your check-ins. It cannot be undone and there is no backup copy kept for you. Deleting a single party takes its address, album and guest list with it.

Security

Every table has row-level security switched on, addresses and albums are gated by the database rather than the interface, and the app is served over HTTPS only. No system is perfect, so please tell us straight away if something looks wrong.

Age

Turnout is for people 16 and over. If you are under 18, you need a parent or guardian’s permission to use it. We do not knowingly keep accounts for anyone under 16 — if we find one, we delete it. A parent or guardian can email us to have their child’s account and everything in it removed.

Your rights

You can see and edit your profile in the app at any time, and delete everything in two taps. If you want a copy of what is stored about you, or want something corrected, email and ask — we will do it.

Changes

If this policy changes in a way that matters, the app will show it to you again and ask you to accept it before you carry on.

Terms of Service · last updated 31 August 2026

These are the rules for using Turnout. Making an account means you agree to them.

Who can use Turnout

You need to be 16 or older, and if you are under 18 you need a parent or guardian’s permission. One account per person, it is yours alone, and you are responsible for what happens on it. Keep your password to yourself.

Turnout does not run the parties

This is the important one. Turnout is a place to list and find parties. It does not host them, organize them, inspect them, vet the people at them, or take any responsibility for what happens at one. Every party belongs entirely to the person who posted it.

If you host: it is your house, your guests and your responsibility — including following the law, and not serving alcohol to anyone underage. If you go: you are going at your own risk. Use your judgment, tell someone where you are going, and look out for each other.

Money

Turnout does not process payments and never touches your money. If a party has a cover, it is collected in person by the host and settled between the two of you. The door tools in the app are a private tally for the host, not a payment system. Turnout will not referee a dispute about cover.

Things you cannot do
  • Post a party that is not real, or one you are not actually throwing.
  • Post anyone’s address without their permission.
  • Post photos of people who do not want to be posted.
  • Harass, threaten, bully or impersonate anyone.
  • Sell alcohol, drugs or anything illegal — or use Turnout to arrange it.
  • Break into, scrape, overload or reverse-engineer the app.
What you post stays yours

Your photos and messages belong to you. By putting them in Turnout you give us permission to store and display them to the people the app is meant to show them to — nothing wider, and nothing outside the app. Delete them and that permission ends.

Reporting and removal

Every profile and party has a report option, and blocking someone cuts contact both ways. We can remove any party, photo or message, and suspend or delete any account, if it breaks these rules or puts people at risk — usually with a warning, immediately if someone could get hurt.

Ending it

You can delete your account whenever you want, from your profile. We can close an account that breaks these rules.

No promises about the service

Turnout is provided as-is. It is a young app run by one person, so it may go down, lose something, or get something wrong. We do not guarantee it will be available, accurate, or free of bugs.
